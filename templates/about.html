SpamShield

"Smarter Email. Safer Inbox."

SpamShield is a full-stack, portfolio-grade Email and Message Spam Detection system built with Python, Scikit-learn, Flask, and a modern SaaS UI. It leverages supervised machine learning—specifically Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency (TF-IDF) feature engineering coupled with a Multinomial Naive Bayes probabilistic classifier—to identify spam with real-world empirical validation.

End-to-End Machine Learning Pipeline

How SpamShield transforms raw message text into reliable classification verdicts

1

Data Cleaning & Policy

Raw data is parsed using robust fallback encodings. Missing values are discarded and exact duplicates are pruned (keeping first occurrence) to prevent test-set data contamination.

2

Stratified 80/20 Split

The dataset is partitioned into 4,128 training examples and 1,032 test examples using stratification to preserve natural class balance across both sets.

3

TF-IDF Vectorization

Converts text into numerical feature vectors using 1-to-2 word n-grams, sublinear frequency scaling, and English stop-word filtering. Fit strictly on training data only.

4

Multinomial Naive Bayes

Applies Bayes' theorem with multinomial word frequency likelihoods and additive Laplace smoothing (α=0.1) to compute posterior class probabilities.

Real-World Considerations & Limitations

While SpamShield demonstrates portfolio-quality engineering and achieves over 98% accuracy on held-out test data, real-world email security operates in an adversarial environment:

  • Domain & Dataset Scope: The model is trained on the SMS Spam Collection dataset. While vocabulary overlaps significantly with spam emails (e.g. prizes, urgent alerts, account flags), enterprise email spam often includes multi-paragraph prose, legitimate disclaimers, or obfuscated characters.
  • Header & Authentication Signals: True email security systems (e.g. Google Workspace, Microsoft Defender) evaluate sender authentication protocols such as SPF, DKIM, and DMARC, IP reputation, and TLS encryption, in addition to text body analysis.
  • Attachments & URLs: Text classifiers do not scan executable files, PDFs, or live phishing URLs for malware payloads.
  • Statistical Nature: A prediction is an estimation of likelihood, not an absolute guarantee. Suspicious messages should always be treated with healthy skepticism.
